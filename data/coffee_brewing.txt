Pour-over coffee is a manual brewing method where hot water, typically between 92 and 96 degrees Celsius, is poured slowly and evenly over ground coffee sitting in a paper or metal filter. The water drips through the grounds and filter into a container below, extracting oils and flavor compounds along the way.

Grind size matters a lot: a finer grind increases the surface area in contact with water, so it extracts faster and needs a shorter total brew time, while a coarser grind needs more time to avoid under-extraction. Most pour-over recipes start with a 30-second "bloom," where a small amount of water is poured over the grounds first to let trapped carbon dioxide escape, which improves even extraction during the rest of the pour.

A common starting ratio is about 1 gram of coffee to 16 grams of water, though this varies by taste and roast.
