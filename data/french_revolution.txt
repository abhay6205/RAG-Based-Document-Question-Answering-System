The French Revolution began in 1789, driven by a combination of financial crisis, widespread food shortages, and growing resentment toward the privileges of the aristocracy and clergy under King Louis XVI. The storming of the Bastille on July 14, 1789 became a symbolic turning point and is still celebrated as France's national day.

The revolution led to the abolition of the monarchy, the rise of the National Assembly, and the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen, which asserted principles of liberty, equality, and popular sovereignty. It later descended into the Reign of Terror between 1793 and 1794, led by Maximilien Robespierre, during which tens of thousands of people were executed, often by guillotine, on suspicion of counter-revolutionary activity.

The revolution reshaped not just France but the political landscape of Europe, inspiring both democratic movements and, eventually, the rise of Napoleon Bonaparte, who took power in 1799.
